## Prompt

> The Pydantic v2 schema in `packages/eval/schema.py` defines `GoldCase`, the unit of a 50–100-case evaluation gold standard for a UK housing-tribunal AI mediator. Cases are 2019–2024, OCR'd from public tribunal PDFs, annotated by a paralegal and stored as JSONL. Eight cross-field invariants are enforced (INV-1..INV-8 in `docs/eval/gold-schema.md`).
>
> Identify the failure modes you would expect this schema to hit under noisy real-world tribunal text. Specifically:
> 1. Where could an annotator legitimately be unable to produce a valid case?
> 2. Where is the schema too strict?
> 3. Where is the schema not strict enough?
> 4. What field is missing?
> 5. Examiner attack surface for the thesis.

## Codex response (verbatim)

**Q1.** An annotator can legitimately be unable to produce a valid `GoldCase` when the tribunal gives only a single global award, because `ground_truth_outcome.per_issue` is mandatory and `total_awarded_gbp` must equal its sum. Mixed cleaning/damages/disrepair decisions also do not fit cleanly because `claim_type` is single-valued, so one real case can require a lossy collapse into one label. Cases with mirrored tenant and landlord monetary positions around the same deposit pot are also awkward, because the schema has no canonical dispute-value field and derives `case_size` from the raw sum of `claimed_amounts`. OCR-damaged or partially redacted judgments make required reasoning quotes and paragraph references difficult to supply without invention.

**Q2.** The schema is too strict where it requires per-issue decomposition, a single `claim_type`, and a `case_size` derived mechanically from summed `claimed_amounts`. Real decisions can be validly annotated yet still fail if they use page references instead of paragraph references, if they award a lump sum without breaking it down, or if both sides describe overlapping money claims differently. The hard 2019–2024 date window is acceptable for a pilot corpus, but it rejects otherwise useful validation cases by construction rather than by substantive relevance. The required 50-character `facts` summary also forces paraphrase even where the source text is sparse or heavily redacted.

**Q3.** The schema is not strict enough in several places: it never checks that `overall_winner` is consistent with per-issue winners, or that `winner="split"` corresponds to any meaningful split outcome. It also cannot catch fabricated `paragraph_ref` values, invented `facts` summaries, duplicate issue labels, or empty `evidence` and `statutory_basis` lists. INV-5 only enforces string matching between issue labels, so it will miss drift such as duplicated issues, over-broad issue buckets, or semantically dubious but textually matching labels. As written, a JSON object can validate while still being a weak or misleading gold-standard case.

**Q4.** The most important missing fields are structured authority citations with dates, a canonical dispute or deposit amount independent of issue labels, and source-location provenance at page or span level. Without authority dates, the plan's temporal-leakage audit is not computable from the schema; without a canonical dispute value, `case_size` can be wrong in mirrored-claim cases. Missing representation-detail fields also limit later fairness analysis by advocacy type or counsel, even if you do not store full names. An annotation-uncertainty field should also be added so the reviewer can mark a case as valid but weak because of OCR noise, redaction, or genuine ambiguity.

**Q5.** The first structural hole an examiner will poke is label validity, not raw sample size. They will ask whether the schema can faithfully represent mixed-type cases, unapportioned awards, and noisy OCR provenance, because those design choices directly determine the reported accuracy, Brier, hallucination, and ablation numbers. The next attack is annotation reliability, since the plan describes paralegal sign-off but not double annotation or adjudication. The third attack is metric computability, because some promised audits — especially temporal leakage and unsupported-claim rate — are under-specified relative to what the schema actually stores.

**[1] Severity: HIGH**
File: `.sisyphus/plans/track-a-plan.md:57`; `packages/eval/schema.py:119-120`; `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:60-66`
Issue: The plan says `dataset.audit()` will reject train cases citing authorities after `2022-12-31`, but the schema stores no authority dates or case-citation metadata, so that leakage audit is not computable from the gold set.
Fix: Add structured authority/case citation fields with dates, or redefine the leakage audit around metadata that `GoldCase` already captures.

**[2] Severity: HIGH**
File: `packages/eval/schema.py:85-98`; `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:88-90`
Issue: A tribunal decision that awards only a global figure and gives no per-issue apportionment cannot validate because `per_issue` is mandatory and `total_awarded_gbp` must equal its sum.
Fix: Add an explicit unapportioned/global-outcome path, such as a nullable breakdown or a dedicated `global_award_reason`.

**[3] Severity: HIGH**
File: `packages/eval/schema.py:114`; `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:32,168`
Issue: The single-valued `claim_type` field forces lossy labeling of real mixed-type cases even though the documentation already acknowledges that one case can hit multiple claim types.
Fix: Change `claim_type` to `list[ClaimType]` or add a primary/secondary taxonomy before freezing `v1`.

**[4] Severity: HIGH**
File: `packages/eval/schema.py:74-98,121-123`; `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:80-90,109-112`
Issue: Nothing enforces consistency between `overall_winner`, per-issue winners, and award amounts, so internally contradictory outcomes can validate and silently corrupt accuracy labels.
Fix: Add invariants that define the semantics of `tenant`, `landlord`, and `split` and validate `overall_winner` against the per-issue aggregate.

**[5] Severity: HIGH**
File: `.sisyphus/plans/track-a-plan.md:23-25,70`; `packages/eval/schema.py:118-123`; `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:36-41`
Issue: The planned unsupported-claim-rate and citation-entailment metrics are not reproducible from this schema because it contains no atomic claim units or claim-to-support links, only a free-text `facts` summary plus loose evidence and quote lists.
Fix: Add claim-level annotations with supporting paragraph references and explicit support-status labels.

**[6] Severity: HIGH**
File: `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:3,157-163`; `.sisyphus/plans/track-a-plan.md:37-39`
Issue: The versioning policy freezes `v1` on the first reviewer-signed-off case even though phase 3 is only a 10-case pilot and the docs already list unresolved schema limitations.
Fix: Delay the `v1` freeze until after the pilot annotation batch and a formal schema-review checkpoint.

**[7] Severity: HIGH**
File: `.sisyphus/plans/track-a-plan.md:39,42,54-55`; `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:21`
Issue: The plan requires paralegal sign-off but does not require double annotation, adjudication, or any agreement measure, leaving the gold labels open to subjectivity attacks.
Fix: Add a blind second-review sample, an adjudication log, and a pre-specified agreement metric (e.g. Cohen's kappa).

**[8] Severity: HIGH**
File: `.sisyphus/plans/track-a-plan.md:13,40,67-72`
Issue: The plan commits thesis claims like accuracy `>70%` and Brier `<0.20` as point estimates but gives no confidence-interval, bootstrap, or minimum-support plan, so the numbers can be attacked as unstable on a 50-case corpus.
Fix: Pre-specify uncertainty reporting and minimum effective test-set sizes for each metric before phase 4/6 execution.

**[9] Severity: MEDIUM**
File: `packages/eval/schema.py:68-71,155-166`; `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:31,72-74,111`; `.sisyphus/plans/track-a-plan.md:44`
Issue: `case_size` is derived from the raw sum of all `claimed_amounts` across parties, which can double-count mirrored claim/counterclaim descriptions of the same underlying deposit dispute.
Fix: Store a canonical dispute-value or deposit-value field and derive `case_size` from that rule instead of summing all party claims.

**[10] Severity: MEDIUM**
File: `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:30,167`; `.sisyphus/plans/track-a-plan.md:44`
Issue: `region` is free text even though it is used in the stratification target, so spelling variants like `London` versus `Greater London` can silently break sampling audits.
Fix: Normalize `region` to a closed enum now and keep the raw source string separately if needed.

**[11] Severity: MEDIUM**
File: `packages/eval/schema.py:119-120`; `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:37-38`; `.sisyphus/plans/track-a-plan.md:13,23-24,70`
Issue: A `GoldCase` can validate with empty `evidence` and empty `statutory_basis`, which is too permissive for a corpus meant to support citation precision and unsupported-claim measurement.
Fix: Require at least one source-bearing item or an explicit `evidence_unavailable_reason` that downstream metrics can treat separately.

**[12] Severity: MEDIUM**
File: `packages/eval/schema.py:56-65,80-82`; `docs/eval/gold-schema.md:58,66,97`
Issue: Provenance is weak because `paragraph_ref` is optional for evidence and statutes and completely unstructured even when required for quotes, so fabricated or unlocatable references will pass validation under noisy OCR.
Fix: Replace bare `paragraph_ref` strings with structured provenance fields (page, paragraph, text-span) and validate them in the annotation workflow.
