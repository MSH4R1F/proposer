## Prompt

> The Pydantic v2 schema in `packages/eval/schema.py` defines `GoldCase`, the unit of a 50–100-case evaluation gold standard for a UK housing-tribunal AI mediator. Cases are 2019–2024, OCR'd from public tribunal PDFs, annotated by a paralegal and stored as JSONL. Eight cross-field invariants are enforced (INV-1..INV-8 in `docs/eval/gold-schema.md`).
>
> Identify the failure modes you would expect this schema to hit under noisy real-world tribunal text. Specifically:
>
> 1. **Where could an annotator legitimately be unable to produce a valid case?** (e.g. multi-issue cases that don't fit the per-issue list cleanly; partially redacted decisions; cases where the tribunal awards a global figure without per-issue breakdown.)
> 2. **Where is the schema too strict?** Will real cases fail validation for reasons that are *not* annotation errors?
> 3. **Where is the schema not strict enough?** What annotation drift will INV-1..INV-8 fail to catch? (e.g. a `winner=split` outcome with `awarded_gbp` that doesn't reflect any actual split; quotes whose `paragraph_ref` is fabricated.)
> 4. **What field is missing?** What downstream metric will silently be impossible because we didn't capture some piece of metadata at annotation time? Examples to consider: counsel name (for fairness audit), tribunal panel composition, prior settlement attempts, deposit amount independent of any one issue, was the deposit protected and where, was the s.21 notice valid, did the tenancy continue beyond the dispute.
> 5. **Examiner attack surface for the thesis.** If an examiner has 30 minutes with this schema and the corpus, what is the first hole they'll poke? "Your sample is too small" is a given — what *structural* attack do you predict?
>
> Return your analysis as numbered findings with severity (high / medium / low) and a one-sentence proposed remediation per finding.
